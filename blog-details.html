The Complete Winter Plumbing Protection Guide for UK Homeowners

With temperatures dropping across the UK, your plumbing faces its biggest annual challenge. Frozen pipes can burst and cause thousands of pounds in damage — but almost every case is completely preventable.

David Harris
Founder & Master Plumber · January 20, 2026

Why Winter Is Your Plumbing's Biggest Threat

Every year, the Association of British Insurers estimates that frozen and burst pipes cost homeowners in the UK over £1 billion in damage. Yet the vast majority of these incidents are entirely preventable with basic preparation and awareness.

When water freezes inside a pipe, it expands — and unlike most materials, it doesn't compress. This expansion creates enormous pressure inside the pipe, often far exceeding what the material was designed to handle. The result? A catastrophic burst, often in a hidden location, releasing hundreds of litres of water before you even notice.

The 7 Essential Steps to Protect Your Pipes

1. Insulate Exposed Pipes

Pipes in unheated spaces — loft spaces, garages, under floors, in external walls — are most at risk. Lagging (pipe insulation foam) is inexpensive, available at any DIY store, and takes minutes to fit. Focus on:

  • Pipes in the loft and attic space
  • Pipes in the garage or utility room
  • Any pipes on external walls
  • Outdoor taps and garden hose connections

2. Know Where Your Stopcock Is

Your mains stopcock — the valve that controls all water entering your home — is usually found under the kitchen sink or near the front door. In a freeze emergency, turning this off immediately can save thousands of pounds in damage. Make sure everyone in your household knows where it is and how to turn it.

"In the first 10 minutes after a pipe bursts, most homeowners spend precious time looking for the stopcock. Know it before you need it." — David Harris, FlowMaster

3. Keep Your Heating On During Cold Spells

You don't need to heat your home to 21°C constantly, but during periods of sustained freezing temperatures, setting your thermostat to a minimum of 12–15°C — even overnight and when you're out — prevents pipes from reaching freezing point.

Smart thermostats like Nest or Hive make this simple by allowing you to monitor and control your heating remotely.

4. Let Taps Drip in Extreme Cold

During extreme cold (below -5°C), allowing a slow drip from taps connected to vulnerable pipes can prevent freezing. Moving water is significantly harder to freeze than static water. Even a slow drip maintains enough flow to prevent ice formation.

5. Drain Outdoor Water Systems

Before the first frost, ensure you:

  • Disconnect and drain garden hoses
  • Turn off and drain outdoor tap connections
  • Drain garden irrigation systems
  • Empty water features and fountains

6. Service Your Boiler Before Winter

An annual boiler service isn't just about safety — it also ensures your heating system is working at full efficiency heading into the coldest months. A poorly maintained boiler that fails in January is both expensive and dangerous.

7. Know the Warning Signs of Freezing

Act quickly if you notice:

  • No water coming from a tap during cold weather
  • Frost on exposed pipes
  • Strange smells from pipes (gas build-up)
  • Visible bulging or cracking in pipe walls

What to Do If a Pipe Freezes

If you suspect a pipe has frozen but hasn't yet burst:

  • Turn off your mains stopcock immediately
  • Gently warm the frozen section using a warm (not hot) cloth or a hairdryer on the lowest setting
  • Never use a blowtorch or open flame
  • Open the nearest tap so water can flow as it thaws
  • Call FlowMaster if you're unsure — we offer same-day emergency assessment
